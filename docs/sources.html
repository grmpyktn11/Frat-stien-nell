Method

  1. Names come from Wikipedia's List of people named in the Epstein files. That list covers the flight logs, the contact book, the court filings and the DOJ/Congressional releases.
  2. Filter: public figures only. Victims, survivors and Epstein's staff are removed, along with private individuals who have no public role.
  3. Ivy League: a person counts as attended when Wikidata's "educated at" lists the school, when their Wikipedia article is in the school's alumni category, or when a checked source says so. Graduate and professional schools count (Wharton, Harvard Law, Weill Cornell). Faculty, fellows and trustees are marked separately. Honorary degrees don't count.
  4. Fraternities: Wikidata "member of" entries that are fraternities or sororities, plus article text naming one. Every match was checked by hand. Honor societies such as Phi Beta Kappa and Kappa Beta Phi don't count.
  5. People without a Wikipedia article were researched from professional bios and news coverage. Education is listed only when a source states it.
  6. Web: people are linked to the colleges and fraternities they attended or joined. Graduate schools are grouped under their parent university. A non-Ivy college appears only when two or more people share it.
  7. Being named in these documents does not imply wrongdoing.

Data sources

  • Wikipedia — List of people named in the Epstein files
  • Wikidata — educated at (P69) and member of (P463)
  • Each person's English Wikipedia article: categories and text
  • Professional bios and news reporting for people without an article (listed per person below)
